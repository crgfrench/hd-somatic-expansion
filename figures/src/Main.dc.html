Repeat expansion diseases: normal and disease-causing repeat lengths

Lengths a person is born with, in repeat units, on a logarithmic scale. For Huntington's disease the disease-causing range starts at 36, a threshold known since the gene was found in 1993. Forty or more always leads to the disease; 36 to 39 carries a reduced risk that rises with age, and some carriers never develop it. The dashed line at 150 is a different measurement, made on a single neuron decades later: by then the repeat in that cell has grown well beyond what the person inherited, and cells in one brain differ widely. Above about 150 in a given cell, that cell begins to change. Only Huntington's disease has been measured this way.

A longer repeat at birth puts more neurons past 150 within a lifetime

The repeat a person inherits is the same in every cell at birth. From then on each cell’s copy grows on its own, slowly at first and then faster once it passes about 90. A longer inherited length shortens the slow part, so more of that person’s neurons reach 150 during a lifetime, and symptoms start earlier.
